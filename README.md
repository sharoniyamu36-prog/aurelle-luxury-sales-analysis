# Aurelle Luxury Collective — Sales Analysis Using Excel

## Project Overview

This project analyses sales data for Aurelle Luxury Collective, a simulated luxury retail business. The analysis focuses on product demand, brand performance, category trends, luxury tiers, and product returns.

The objective was to transform raw sales data into meaningful business insights that could help management identify products and brands to prioritise, investigate, or review.

**Tool used:** Microsoft Excel

## Business Problem

Management needs a clearer understanding of how its product range performs across different categories, brands, and luxury tiers.

The analysis was designed to investigate six key questions:

1. Which five products have the highest total quantity sold?
2. Which product categories are growing or declining each year?
3. Which brands sell the most units, and how has their ranking changed?
4. Which months have the highest demand for each product category?
5. How does demand differ between Entry Luxury, Core Luxury, and Ultra Luxury products?
6. Which products and categories have the highest unit return rates?

## Dataset and Preparation

The original dataset contained 8,647 records and 44 columns.

I reviewed the business requirements and selected 19 relevant columns for the analysis, focusing on product information, brands, categories, luxury tiers, dates, quantities, pricing, discounts, costs, and returns.

The preparation process included checking for missing values, duplicate records, inconsistent text, invalid dates, and potential data quality issues.

This helped create a more focused dataset for analysis.

## Data Analysis Process

### 1. Data Cleaning
- Selected the columns relevant to the business questions.
- Standardised text fields and checked data types.
- Investigated missing values and duplicate records.
- Validated quantities, dates, prices, and return information.

### 2. Calculated Columns

I created calculated columns to support the analysis:

- Gross Sales
- Discount Amount
- Sales After Discount
- Returned Sales Value
- Net Sales
- Net Quantity Sold
- Total Cost
- Gross Profit
- Order Year
- Order Month

These calculations supported the evaluation of sales performance, product demand, profitability, and returns.

### 3. KPI Development

The dashboard includes key performance indicators for:

- Total Revenue
- Discount Amount
- Sales After Discounts
- Returned Sales
- Net Sales
- Total Cost
- Total Profit
- Quantity Sold
- Quantity Returned
- Total Orders

### 4. Exploratory Analysis and Visualisation

I used Excel PivotTables, charts, and slicers to explore brand performance, category sales, luxury-tier demand, monthly trends, and product returns.

The interactive dashboard allows users to explore performance across years, brands, product categories, and luxury tiers.

## Key Findings

- **Net Sales:** Approximately ₦8.59 billion after discounts and returned sales.
- **Leading Brand:** Maison Aurelle recorded the highest overall unit sales, with 3,185 units.
- **Luxury Tier:** Core Luxury accounted for approximately 50% of total units sold.
- **Leading Categories by Net Sales:** Jewelry and Watches recorded the highest category sales values in the dashboard.

These findings provide an initial view of sales performance and identify areas for further investigation.

Further analysis of year-over-year category growth, annual brand rankings, category-specific monthly demand, and unit return rates can provide more detailed answers to the business questions.

## Business Recommendations

Based on the initial findings, management could:

1. Review the sales performance of leading products and investigate changes in demand.
2. Monitor brand rankings and identify brands gaining or losing market position.
3. Evaluate inventory allocation across luxury tiers and product categories.
4. Investigate products and categories with elevated return rates.
5. Combine sales performance with profitability and return-rate analysis before making inventory or product-range decisions.

These are preliminary recommendations. Final decisions should be supported by validated calculations and the detailed results of each analysis.
